Executive Summary

This guidebook has been prepared to assist students, faculty, internship advisors, and agency
(industry) supervisors in understanding the purposes, objectives, responsibilities, policies and
requirements for the internship programme of Limkokwing University.

Against the threat of a declining world economy, the search is on for plans of action that will cushion
the harsh effects of a global meltdown in terms of increasing unemployment and slower rates of
growth. Emerging economies will be particularly vulnerable because of their over dependence on
exports of their raw materials or manufactured goods to the developed world. When the demand for
their goods and services declines sharply, millions will be out of jobs. Economic recovery may take
several years and in the meantime, emerging economies must take immediate measures to become
self-reliant and seek new and innovative ways to cushion the adverse economic and economic
effects on the people.

As a developing country, Sierra Leone is not immune from the effects of the economic fallout. It must
brace itself to meet the formidable challenges ahead and put plans and policies in place to offset the
negative consequences of an unstable financial and economic environment.

Education is an important key to early economic recovery because it will improve the knowledge and
skills of the people so that they can be either retrained to take advantage of new opportunities and to
also start off as new and innovative entrepreneurs.

In this effort, the Limkokwing University of Creative Technology is ready to play its part. It is
introducing the Limkokwing Entrepreneurship Acceleration Platform (LEAP) for Sierra Leone as a
value-added strategy to develop entrepreneurship skills. LEAP is internally known as the Internship
Programme.

LEAP is tailor-made to suit the pressing needs of Limkokwing graduates to acquire the necessary
skills and experience to become entrepreneurs on their own, as well as enhance their employability in
the marketplace.

History and Current Situation

Sierra Leone achieved independence on the 27th of April 1961. The country attained republican
status on the 19th April 1971. Since independence many changes have been experienced politically
and economically and in the social society of Sierra Leone.

1. Programme Introduction

Internship is a work experience programme characterized by intentional learning. During this
experience, a student assumes a responsible role in an organization and actively reflects on what he
or she is observing and learning. It is important that the student links their academic studies with the
world of work.

Three important elements distinguish internship programme from a short-term job or volunteer work:

   the academic background which the intern brings to the practical experience;
   active reflection during the internship; and
   the final project which demonstrates the learning accomplished as it relates to the student's
   academic discipline.

Internship can take place in a variety of settings, from corporations, banks, publishing houses, and
marketing firms to television studios, human service agencies and research institutes. Some students
may receive remuneration while others may not.

This Twelve-week (12) experience is consolidated by way of in-practice supervision and a training
report. The internship program occurs in the final semester of all degrees. Students who have fulfilled
all course requirements up to and including all specified requirements from semester one to five, for
any degree, are eligible to undergo internship.

LEAP runs two types of internship programme:

   an external placement within the industry; and  within an acceleration platform based
  at the University

Students have a choice either to be placed as interns within the industry or enrol in the acceleration
programme which will be dealing with real life industry projects still interacting with the industry
practitioners.

The difference is that in the external internship, the students will be involved in projects handled by
the industry; while in the acceleration programme they will produce or execute projects in line and in
some cases in conjunction with the existing industry.

Both options will fulfil the requirements to prepare the students and meet the objectives of
internship training.

  The structure of the course and assessment criteria is the same for both options
  (External and In-house placement).

2. Objectives & Expected Outcomes of LEAP

The Limkokwing Entrepreneurship Acceleration Platform (LEAP) has the following objectives:

   To develop young, confident and empower young entrepreneurs who will create new job
   opportunities and thus take the national economy several notches up the value chain.
   To provide industry with ready and fully employable graduates who will, in time, take on leadership
   roles in industry.

Specifically, it will:

   Provide training in entrepreneurship development that will fully equip Limkokwing students to
   conceptualise and develop commercial enterprises.
   Provide students with skills to master a business from ideation, drawing up business plans, and
   implementing them.
   Help students hone their skills through guided presentation of their plans and work to Faculty as
   well as to Industry Advisors.
   Empower students with the confidence to chart their own future in the Sierra Leone business
   environment as well as test and develop themselves and their ideas in the region.
   Complement the Sierra Leone Government's efforts to encourage creation of more innovative
   entrepreneurs.

The internship program provides a planned transition from the University Curriculum to a professional
industry setting. During the internship experience, the student will test the practical application of the
theories of academic learning in the professional setting under the guidance and supervision of both
a professional staff member and a university faculty advisor.

4. The Course Overview

4.1 Admission into the Internship Programme
For a student to enroll for internship the following conditions apply:

 a. Students who have fulfilled all course requirements are eligible to proceed for internship.

 b. A student with one failed minor module may be allowed to proceed for internship either In-house
 or external placement subject to the approval of the Head of Faculty (The Registration Regulations,
 progression criteria item 6).

 c. The student will not be allowed to retake a pending module and at the same time with internship
 (In-house or External placement).

 d. A minimum GPA of 2.00 prior to and during Internship enrolment is required. Students on
 academic probation may NOT enroll for internship.

 e. Exemptions from internship can be granted to students with sufficient professional experience in
 their chosen academic subject to approval of the Pro Vice Chancellor, Academic.

4.7 Assessment
Internship programme has two (2) modules: Practical Internship and Internship Report.

A student must pass these two modules to be awarded a diploma Degree. This is also a requirement
for some BA degrees (see attached Form E).

At the end of the internship programme, the student is required to submit a detailed report of the
training, a work diary (Log Book) and all other forms. In the case of students whose professions are
regulated by a registration board (e.g. architecture) they also have to submit a log book to the
academic supervisor. Faculties like Design and Innovation and Creative Multimedia may require their
interns to submit their portfolios.

4.8 Work Diary / Log Book
In the course of the internship students are required to maintain a work diary to record their activities
on a working day such as:

   Appointment notes;
   Discussions and meeting decisions;
   Instructions received from supervisors and seniors;  instructions given to others;
   and actions taken by the student.

In a professional work environment, the interns may be given a number of assignments to handle and
this requires them to meet, consult and report to various people. Therefore, the ability to maintain a
clear record of the sequence of events while carrying out the tasks or the project is important.

6. Limitations

It is recognized that students' existing skills may not be completely or wholly aligned with those
required by the Industry. The students' academic mentors and industry supervisors will endeavor to
identify and fill these gaps.

We also recognize that, in industry the notion of accountability, professionalism and responsibility
may vary with different positions and organizations. This results in differences of incentives and
motivation to learn, therefore, these differences should be taken into account during appraisals.

The students are however expected to act as if they have entered the workplace as a member of
staff. This will be reflected by their adherence to the training guidelines and rules as determined by
the University.

Both points above recognize explicitly the sense of `artificiality' that can arise in internship programs.
This is fostered by the fact that the internship programme runs for only three (3) months and lack of
economic reward. From the perspective of the industry the student is a `visitor' or at best `an extra
pair of hands' - that is, they are there on a learning experience rather than acquiring skills.

7. Internship Etiquette

Etiquette refers to:

  the forms, manners, and ceremonies established by convention as acceptable or required
 in society, in a profession, or in official life

  the rules for such forms, manners, and ceremonies.

Therefore Internship etiquette is behaviour and manners which is acceptable at your internship site.

For many of you, your internship experience will be the first time you have ventured forth into the
world beyond the lecture room. All the rules are different there. People don't expect you to be a
student, they expect you to act the way they do, as a professional in their line of work. You will rarely
find these rules spelled out for you. If you don't figure out the rules, you can have a poor experience
and a poor evaluation.

Here are a few guidelines and ideas to help you get on track:

 Follow the chain of command. It is important for you to know the formal and informal reporting
 structures within your organization. Once you understand them, follow them! The unspoken rule is
 this: do not go around, behind or over anyone. Follow the chain of command in all your
 communications and actions. That means go to your site supervisor first.

Internship GuideBook -- Limkokwing Entrepreneurship Acceleration Platform  Page 15 of 25

[[page:16]]
Respect confidentiality. You can talk about issues, projects, and the work environment, but refrain
from talking about people. Gossip can get back to people and wind up hurting you. Don't be hurt if
you are left out of certain discussions - some issues are for staff ears and eyes only. Finally, don't
take sides; steer clear of interoffice politics. Remember that you are there to work on your project.

Respect the support staff. They have been there longer than you, and they know more than you.
They can be terrific allies in helping you break in, helping you understand the unspoken rules, and
helping you accomplish your goals if you treat them with the respect they are due. Wipe the
thought "just a secretary" out of your mind. Remember this: without support staff, the organization
would not run.

Learn basic social skills. This might seem rather silly, but if no one ever taught you such rituals,
you are well advised to learn them quickly! Go to the library and read some etiquette books, or
pattern your behavior after those around you. How you handle hellos, good-byes, and basic
courtesies of speech and action can win friends or turn people off. For example, don't sit down in
someone's office until you are invited to do so. Keep your feet off the furniture. Hats off inside!
Don't chew gum. In the dining hall, you can get away with reaching across someone for the salt or
pushing your plate out of the way when you are through. In the work world, you lose points.

Attendance and promptness are expected. Because you are a student, some faculty may not
penalize you if you fly into class five minutes late or if you miss class. In the work world, that just
won't cut it. Tardiness and absenteeism signal disrespect for others' time, a lack of interest in the
work. Promptness signals eagerness, responsibility, respect for others. At the beginning of the day
and at all your meetings, be on time or five minutes early. As for being absent from work, serious
illness or family emergencies are the only reasons which may justify absence. It is important to call
immediately and speak directly with your site supervisor if you have a problem which will keep you
from your internship.

Learn to make a positive first impression. Practice until you acquire a firm handshake. Learn
how to make introductions and how to introduce yourself to those you don't know. Be friendly, smile
and extend yourself. These are all parts of those important first impressions which really can earn
you points. Picture this: the Executive Director of your organization is coming down the hall toward
you. You are alone. She is a valuable person to know. Are you ready to introduce yourself?

Dress the part. Yes, this is important! When you walk in the door of your internship site, even if it is
on campus, you are no longer a student. We repeat: you are no longer a student. Appropriate attire
is different for every organization. Look around you. What are others wearing? What about their
hair styles? What kind of accessories are the norm, including earrings for men? Model your dress
and grooming after that of your supervisor and other professional staff, and you cannot go wrong.

Lose the lingo. "Hey, cool!" might be a natural expression of affirmation on campus. In your
internship, it will tell people that you are not yet professional material. Listen to the language of
those around you, listen to your own language, and speak as a professional.

Be a good ambassador. Be cognizant that you reflect the institution. How you perform and
 behave in your internship will affect the future of other Limkokwing interns.

Think about the long-term benefits of good internship etiquette. Many of you will ask your site
supervisors for job recommendations or contacts. Some of you will apply for full-time or summer jobs
at your internship site. Have you proven that you can make it in that type of environment? Have you
earned a positive recommendation? What you do today can stick with you for a long time. Make it
count!
